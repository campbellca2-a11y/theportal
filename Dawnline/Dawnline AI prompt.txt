## Original product concept

Dawnline begins with one object and one proof:

### Stage 1: Sunrise

Watch sunrise traverse the globe.

No birds yet.
No biome logic.
No sound layer.
No clever add-ons.

Just:

**Earth turns.
Light arrives.
Morning moves.**

This is the foundational planetary visualizer.

### Stage 2: Birds

After the sunrise layer works cleanly:

**Light arrives first.
Then the world does sound check.**

Bird activity and sound become a second layer that responds to the arrival of morning.

### Stage 3: Other layers

Only after the foundation is proven may other layers be considered, such as:

* Storms
* Aurora
* Tides
* Migration
* Clouds
* City lights
* Human activity
* Other geographically or temporally driven phenomena

The intended build order is:

| Stage | Layer        | Purpose                                                               |
| ----- | ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Sunrise      | Prove that a planetary wave can be displayed clearly                  |
| 2     | Birds        | Prove that a biological and sound layer can respond to the light wave |
| 3     | Other layers | Extend the same foundation deliberately                               |

Anchor phrase:

**Lights first. Then the sound check.**

“Lights” here primarily means sunlight and the arrival of morning—not city-light decoration.

## Current working conclusion to validate

The current hypothesis is:

* Dawnline v3 contains a legitimate and reusable foundation.
* Its core time, solar-position, globe, shader, and dawn-transition mechanisms are worth preserving.
* It should not be abandoned.
* It should not yet be treated as the final Stage 1 presentation.
* The default visual currently includes more than the original concept requires.
* Sunrise should become the unquestioned primary object.

* Development controls may remain available without being part of the ambient default experience.
* The next pass should refine and simplify the existing build rather than restart from zero.

You must test this conclusion rather than merely repeat it.
